Draw walls that actually join

Proposed drawing sheet · 32 × 32 tiles · 8 columns × 6 rows

Expanded format for review. The current game still uses the old five-tile importer.

1. See the pieces working together

Same wall pieces throughout: top and bottom boundaries, left and right sides, doorway ends, thin partitions, corners and pillars. This is a construction example, not the current game renderer.

2. Inspect every drawing slot

Cream shows the floor beneath transparent pixels; green is the wall cap; dark green is the visible face. The neighbour diagram describes surrounding wall, not floor. Its centre is the tile you are drawing.

3. Draw in this sheet

Use an 8 × 6 grid at native size: 256 × 192 pixels, with no gaps. Slot 00 is floor; 01–47 are distinct wall connections. Draw on a new layer over the construction sheet, then hide the guide layer. Keep labels out of your exported PNG.

Bottom wall: give it a continuous body and cap, rather than the old floating eight-pixel rim. In this proposal exposed south-facing edges have an eight-pixel face. Left and right walls have their own artwork; corner and end pieces complete them. Keep the lighting direction consistent instead of rotating finished wall art.

Use the same layout for the lair and each dungeon theme. Doors, stations, torches and other props stay separate. Extra floor textures can come after the structural set works.
